ACTIVITY 3 · GROUP DISCUSSION

Should tourists visit unspoilt places?

PROTECT THE PLACE
ALLOW RESPONSIBLE TOURISM
1Should tourists be allowed to visit unspoilt places?
2Would you go to Antarctica if you had the opportunity?
1 · THINK Decide your own view.2 · SHARE Give a view + reason + example/consequence.3 · CHALLENGE Ask “Why?” or “What if…?”4 · AGREE Choose the strongest arguments.
1 · POSITIONOn balance, I believe… / From my point of view…
2 · DEVELOPThe main reason is that… / This is particularly important because…
3 · SUPPORTFor example,… / This can lead to… / As a result,…
4 · BALANCEAdmittedly,…; however,… / Although…, I would still argue that…
5 · CONCLUDETherefore, provided that…, … / Overall, I would support…
MOVE TO A NEW GROUP. Give a 45–60 second answer using the B2 frame. A new partner asks one follow-up question. Then compare how your answer improved.
MODEL · QUESTION 1
On balance, I think tourists should be allowed to visit unspoilt places, provided that visitor numbers are strictly controlled. The main reason is that responsible tourism can raise awareness and provide money for conservation and research. For example, carefully managed cruises can teach visitors why Antarctica needs protection. Admittedly, tourism may create pollution and disturb wildlife; however, these risks can be reduced if ships follow strict rules and visitors keep a safe distance from animals. Therefore, I would support limited tourism rather than a complete ban.
MODEL · QUESTION 2
I would definitely consider going to Antarctica because it would be a rare chance to see wildlife and landscapes that are completely different from where I live. That said, I would only choose a tour company that follows environmental regulations. I would also be prepared to accept restrictions, such as staying on marked routes and keeping away from animals, because protecting the area would be more important than getting the perfect photo.

Build a B2 3-day trip plan

Each student develops one question. Then combine the four parts into one coherent group presentation.

MODEL PART: We’d choose Da Nang mainly because it offers both beaches and cultural attractions, which would give the group a good balance of relaxation and sightseeing.
✈ HOW?Choose transport and explain why.We’d travel by… since… Although…, it would…
MODEL PART: We’d travel by plane since it would save a great deal of time. Although it is more expensive than going by coach, it would give us almost a full extra day at the destination.
🏨 STAY?Choose accommodation using clear criteria.We’d stay… which would allow us to…
MODEL PART: We’d book a small hotel near the beach, which would allow us to walk to restaurants in the evening while still being close to the coast.
🎯 DO?Sequence activities across three days.To begin with,… On the second day,… Finally,…
MODEL PART: To begin with, we’d go sightseeing in the city centre. On the second day, we’d take a day trip to Hoi An, and finally we’d spend the last morning relaxing on the beach before checking out.

EXIT TICKET

Four B2 checks

1. After a long voyage, the yacht finally entered the _____ where it could be tied up safely.
Answer: B · harbour — a sheltered place where boats can be moored safely.
2. Our first flight lands in Dubai at midnight, but the connection is not until morning, so we’ll _____ there overnight.
Answer: B · stop over — stay somewhere briefly while travelling to another destination.
3. Which sentence uses travel language naturally?
Answer: C — book a flight and check in are natural travel combinations.
4. Which answer best develops a B2-level argument about tourism in unspoilt areas?
Answer: C — it presents a balanced position, uses a concessive structure, and gives a clear condition.
