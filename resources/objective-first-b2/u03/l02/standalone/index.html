PART 1 · ZOOM IN ON OBLIGATION

Must or have to?

Do not start with a rule. Read each context and decide where the obligation comes from. Then compare your reasoning with a partner.
1. I must remember to renew my passport before I pay for the flight.
speaker himself / herself
The speaker creates the obligation for himself/herself.
2. The doctor says I have to take more exercise before the expedition.
another person / external circumstances
The requirement comes from another person: the doctor.
3. All cars must be left in the designated car park, not on the access road.
rule / official requirement
This is an impersonal rule or notice.
4. Because the airline checks identity at the gate, passengers have to present a valid passport before boarding.
another person / external circumstances
The airline requirement creates the obligation.
5. I must remember to download the boarding pass before I leave home.
speaker himself / herself
This is the speaker’s own strong reminder.
6. Visitors should take waterproof clothing because conditions can change very quickly.
advice rather than obligation
Should expresses a good idea / recommendation, not an obligation.
Use your answers to complete the generalisations. These are useful tendencies, not absolute rules: in real English, must and have to can overlap.

GROUP TASK · 3–4 students. Choose one holiday together. Negotiate one coherent plan and complete every box. Use the target grammar while you decide.
TRANSPORT

How will you travel? What do you have to arrange?

SAMPLE: We would have to book return flights early and arrange an airport transfer because public transport is limited at night.
ACCOMMODATION

Where will you stay? What must it provide?

SAMPLE: We must choose accommodation close to the main attractions, and it should provide secure luggage storage.
FOOD

What should you try? What dietary needs do you have to consider?

SAMPLE: We should try local food, but we would have to check ingredients carefully because one member of the group has an allergy.
ACTIVITIES

What must / should everyone do?

SAMPLE: We must follow the safety instructions, and we should reserve the most popular activities before we arrive.
ENTERTAINMENT

What don’t you have to organise yourselves?

SAMPLE: We don’t have to organise evening entertainment because the resort already offers live music and guided events.
PEOPLE

Who will travel with you or help you during the trip?

SAMPLE: We would travel as a group of four, and we might have to hire a local guide for the more remote areas.
NEGOTIATEI think we should…We probably have to…One thing we must remember is…I don’t think we need to… because…What if we…?
GRAMMAR TARGET Use at least 2 × have to, 2 × must, 1 × don’t have to and 1 × should.
Present a single group plan. Every member contributes, but the presentation should sound connected rather than like four separate mini-talks.
OPENOur group has chosen…
ORGANISEThe first thing we need to consider is… / In terms of transport,…
OBLIGATIONWe would have to… / We must also remember to…
CONTRASTFortunately, we wouldn’t have to…
CONCLUDEOverall, we believe this would be the best option because…
FULL MODEL PRESENTATION
Our group has chosen a skiing holiday in the Alps. The first thing we need to consider is transport, so we would have to book flights and airport transfers in advance. We must also arrange suitable accommodation close to the slopes because we don’t want to spend hours travelling each day. In terms of equipment, everyone would have to wear proper ski clothing, and we must remember to follow the mountain safety rules. Fortunately, we wouldn’t have to organise evening entertainment because the resort already offers restaurants and live music. Overall, we believe this would be the best choice because it combines adventure, comfort and plenty of opportunities to spend time together.
AUDIENCE Which group plan would you actually choose, and why?

Word Formation: think before you build

The exam gives you a base word. Your first decision is not “Which ending looks right?” but What kind of word and meaning does the sentence require?
1Read the whole sentence and text.
2Decide: noun, verb, adjective or adverb?
3Check whether the meaning must be positive or negative.
4Build the word and check spelling.

Their plane’s late ______ was due to a thunderstorm during the flight. ARRIVE

We need a noun after “late”: ARRIVAL.
